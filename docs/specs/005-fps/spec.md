# 005. FPS and frame time of the game in front, and voltages

**Status**: approved

## Why

People who play want to see their frame rate beside their temperatures, and how smooth it is: a steady line is fluid, spikes are stutters. It is the one thing other overlays show that thermi does not. thermi already has a service with the rights to see it (`docs/adr/0003-sensor-service.md`); how it reads frames, and why that way, is `docs/adr/0014-frames-from-etw.md`.

## Requirements

Reading

- R1. Two metrics read the app in front: FPS (`game.fps`), the frames it presented in the last second, as a whole number; and frame time (`game.frametime`), the longest gap between two of its frames that ended in the last second, in milliseconds with one decimal.
- R2. FPS follows the game, not the focus. An app takes over as the game after 3 s in a row in front with a window that fills its monitor (full screen, borderless, maximized, or a window as large as the monitor's work area wherever it sits) while presenting at 30 FPS or more, and faster than the game while that still presents. The game stays it while it presents, even when another window, as a terminal on another monitor, takes the focus: a terminal or a browser that repaints fast for a moment, or slower than the game, never takes over, and once the game stops presenting, the next to meet the rule does. While there is no game, as when thermi or its service starts with a game already running behind, the app in front that meets the rule, or else the fastest app behind with a window that fills its monitor and presents at 30 FPS or more, is the game at once, the 3 s that make an app present (R6) being wait enough. A window of thermi's, or of a process thermi started (the settings window's WebView2), is never the app in front; the one under it is. A program whose frames come from a process it started, as a browser's, counts as itself.
- R3. Frames are read for Direct3D 9 to 12, Vulkan and OpenGL, windowed, borderless and in exclusive full screen, on any GPU, with frame generation's frames counted, and while a capture, as OBS's, records the game.
- R4. A second's values reach the overlay within about two seconds of its end: the service closes each second 0.9 s after it, once the trace has delivered its presents, which it does 0.41 s after them at the median and 0.74 s at most, and no smoothing is added unless the item asks for it.
- R5. An app in front that stops presenting, as a game while it loads, reads 0 FPS and a frame time that grows with the wait up to 999.9 ms, a freeze, for 5 s; after that it presents nothing.
- R6. An app presents once it presented at least 10 frames a second for 3 seconds in a row, so a window that redraws now and then (a terminal or a browser at rest, thermi's own window) presents nothing. While the app in front presents nothing (the desktop, a document), FPS and frame time items take no room in the overlay, and a pill left with nothing shows nothing. While frames cannot be read at all (a service from before, no trace session free), they take no room in the overlay either, so no taskbar shows an FPS of `–` all day, and the window shows them as `–` with why.

Items

- R7. The window offers FPS and Frame time under a device of their own, Game, in the catalog and among the items to add, each with its history, chart, graph and tray icon as any item, labeled FPS.
- R8. FPS and frame time have no lines by default, since a good frame rate differs from game to game, so they show in the neutral color. A line the person adds in the chart comes at 60 FPS for a warning and 30 for critical, or at 50 ms and 100 ms for frame time, or 5 beyond the other line. With lines, FPS warns when it is low, with the critical line below the warning, and frame time when it is high.
- R9. A new settings file's first overlay ends with FPS, so a game shows it with nothing set up. Settings files that exist are left as they are.

The service

- R10. The service reads frames only while a client is connected, and stays within its budget (`docs/adr/0011-sensor-service-memory-budget.md`), 3 % of one core and 80 MB, while a game presents at 144 FPS.
- R11. The pipe stays one-way, and no process's name leaves the service: frames go out as process ids and numbers.
- R12. `thermi-sensors --frames <file> --seconds <n>`, as administrator, records each present of every process, with ids and times and no names, for tests and bug reports.

Voltages

- R13. The voltages the library reads (a CPU's core, SoC and memory controller, the board's +12 V, +5 V and +3.3 V, a GPU's core) leave the service, and the window lists them in the catalog under their device, found by the name the library gives each, as "+3V Standby", or by words such as "volts"; a board whose layout the library does not know names its inputs "Voltage #1" on, and thermi keeps those names, true to what it reads, since which rail an input carries and its divider are the board's: the item can be renamed, and adds them as items, each shown in volts, as `1.248 V` under 10 V and `12.10 V` from 10 V on. They have no lines by default, lines cannot judge them, and they combine as their highest or average, never as a sum; a CPU's voltage of exactly 0, as without the driver, is missing, as its temperature is, and so is each core's VID while the driver may be missing, which then reads the same for every core; no default overlay shows one.

## Design

**The service** (a new folder, `Frames`, of `src/Thermi.SensorHost`) runs one real-time ETW session, `thermi-frames`, through CsWin32, while a client is connected, enabling DXGI's and Direct3D 9's present events and the graphics kernel's for the rest, on a thread of its own that runs `ProcessTrace` (ADR 0014). Each present goes to Core's `FrameTally` (a new folder, `Frames`, of `src/Thermi.Core`), which each second gives, per process that has presented at 10 FPS or more for 3 seconds in a row, its FPS and its frame time, and keeps a process 5 s at 0 FPS after its last present. A runtime's present reaches the kernel too, which may tell of it more than one way, so each source is counted apart and each second takes the one that told of most: a frame told twice counts once, and a game that changes how it presents, as Zwift between a ride (the detailed history) and its menus (`BlitRect`), keeps its count. `FramedSource` wraps the sensors' read and adds the tally's 8 busiest processes, `dwm.exe` left out, as fixed slots of sensors under the hardware type `Frames`: process id, parent's id, FPS, frame time. The slots are always there, without values when the session could not start, so the catalog line never changes with them. The session starts with the first read and stops 5 s after the last. `--frames` writes the presents a session of its own sees, as JSON lines, through the same tally's input, so a recording never stops the service's.

**The readers.** Core's `GameSensors.With(snapshot, front)`, beside `SystemSensors.With`, drops the slots and adds the game's two sensors, `/game/0/framerate/0` and `/game/0/frametime/0`, with the values of the slot whose process, or parent, is `front`, the game's process, and without values when no slot is (the catalog says why: frames that cannot be read, or no game presenting). Core's `FrontGame` picks the game each tick from the slots, the process in front and whether its window fills its monitor (R2), and keeps it while it presents. The overlay and the window find the process in front on their tick: the foreground window's (`GetForegroundWindow`, `GetWindowThreadProcessId`), and when that is thermi's own or of a process thermi started (the settings window's WebView2), the next shown window below it in Z order of another program (`GetWindow` with `GW_HWNDNEXT`); a window fills its monitor when it is maximized (`IsZoomed`) or as large as its work area (`GetWindowRect`, `GetMonitorInfo`); the desktop, the taskbar and File Explorer give none, since Explorer starts nearly every app and a game behind it would match by its parent (`src/Thermi/Windows/FrontApp.cs`, in `SystemFeed`). Everything after takes the game's sensors as any: `SensorMap` maps the two metrics to them, the catalog lists them under Game, the history and lines keep them.

**Kinds and lines.** Core gains the sensor kinds `FrameRate` (frames per second, no decimals) and `FrameTime` (milliseconds, one decimal), `DeviceKind.Game` and `MetricLabel.Fps`. `Threshold` learns that a kind warns low: for `FrameRate` a warning is at or above its critical, and a value at or under a line reaches it; the file's shape does not change, so no settings version is added, and `ConfigRules` checks the order by the kind. Their lines run up to 1000; they have none by default, and `MeterChart.Added` places a line added to them. The window's chart (`src/Thermi.Settings.Web/src/chart`) draws and drags a low kind's lines as it is told by the bridge, the critical below. The texts, Game, FPS, Frame time and their descriptions, join the translations.

**Defaults.** A new settings file's first overlay, `OverlayConfig.First` in `ThermiConfig.New`, is `OverlayConfig.DefaultItems` with FPS after, with no lines; it takes no room until a game is in front (R6), so the overlay a new user sees does not change. `DefaultItems` itself, what an added overlay and the tests start from, does not change.

**Files that change**: the new folders above; `src/Thermi.Core/Sensors/Metrics.cs`, `Reading.cs`, `SensorMap.cs`, `SensorCatalog.cs`; `src/Thermi.Core/Settings/Threshold.cs`, `ConfigRules.cs`, `OverlayConfig.cs`; `src/Thermi.Core/Look/ReadingFormatter.cs`; `src/Thermi.SensorHost/Program.cs`, `SensorCommands.cs`, `SensorHostEnvironment.cs`, `NativeMethods.txt`; `src/Thermi/Windows/SystemFeed.cs`, which both the overlay and the window read through, and `src/Thermi/Overlay/ConfigStore.cs` for a new file; the chart; `AGENTS.md`'s commands; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/QUICKSTART.md`.

**Testing.** The edges are a recording of presents in and what the overlay shows out. A recording is made with `--frames` while the author rides Zwift, his one game, an OpenGL one: into a world, through its loading screen, alt-tabbing to a browser and the desktop and back; trimmed to the game's process and a few others, it replays through `FrameTally` into snapshots, and those through `GameSensors` and the layout with the process in front given, which is the acceptance of R1, R2, R4, R5, R6 and R9 in one table. The low lines are rows of the thresholds' existing table. The session itself, the APIs and the cost are checked by hand on the author's PC: Zwift against its own counter, and the other APIs with free demos that present through them (Vulkan's `vkcube`, Direct3D 9, 11 and 12 samples), against PresentMon run beside them.

**Test budget.** This spec adds at most 10 unique tests; most of its cases are rows of tables that exist.

**Voltages.** Core gains the sensor kind `Voltage`, which `SensorMap` takes from the library's type of the same name, so the service stops dropping voltages and the catalog, its search, the items and the charts take them as any kind: `ReadingFormatter` shows them in volts with three decimals under 10 V and two from there, they take no offset, since a board's divider errs by a factor and not by a sum, and `Threshold.CanMeter` leaves them out, so they have no lines. The window's catalog adds a chip for them, and their name and search words join the translations. Their test is the author's desktop recorded with and without admin rights: as administrator, its ITE IT8689E's nine inputs, each core's VID and both GPUs' voltages; without, no board, and every core's VID at the same 1.55 V, which `SystemSensors.With`, the step every reader takes, sends as 0 while `SensorMap.DriverMayBeMissing` holds, so the catalog tells it as the driver's.

**Rejected:** a frame-time graph of every frame, as some overlays draw, sent from the service: the pipe and the history are one value a second, and the longest frame of each second already shows every stutter as a spike in the item's line, at no cost.
