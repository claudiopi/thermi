# Thermi.Core

Everything thermi decides without Windows: snapshots and their line format, metrics, the sensor map, formatting, thresholds, the settings file, its edits and their undo, the overlay's layout, look and place as plain data, OLED care, the items' power options and their history, saved styles and the style file, when each overlay shows, what the tray icons show, the translations, the throttle that paces writes and repaints, and the settings window's sensor catalog, history and fan finder, and the hotkeys and their status.

Each topic is a folder, and its namespace: `Sensors` (snapshots, metrics, the sensor map, the catalog, history and fan finder), `Settings` (the file's records, JSON, migration, rules, ids and thresholds), `Editing` (the edits, their gate and undo), `Look` (layout, look, formatting, styles and item history), `Placement` (snaps, monitor ids, OLED care and visibility), `Pipe` (the sensor pipe's lines: the whole snapshot once and on any change of its sensors, then its values alone), `Tray` and `Translations`. `Hotkeys.cs`, `Throttle.cs` and `WindowRequest.cs` serve both processes across topics and stay at the root.

- **Depends on nothing**, Windows included. Its target framework is `net10.0`, and `RepoRulesTests` keeps it that way. If you need a Windows API, it belongs in `src/Thermi` or `src/Thermi.SensorHost`.
- **Keeps 90 % line and branch coverage**, checked by `scripts/check.ps1`.
- **Tested from recordings** in `tests/Thermi.Tests`: real snapshots in, what the overlay shows out.
- **Time** comes from an injected `TimeProvider`, as in `LatestSnapshot`, `Throttle`, `SensorHistory`, `ItemHistory`, `BusiestDrive` and `FanFinder`.
- **The settings file** is version 2: `Settings/OverlayConfig.cs` and `Settings/StyleConfig.cs` hold its fields, `Settings/ThermiConfigJson.cs` reads and writes it and tells a file cut short, as while an editor saves, from a broken one, whose `Settings/ConfigProblem.cs` says what, where and why, `Settings/ConfigMigration.cs` turns a version 1 file into it, `Settings/ItemIds.cs` gives every item its id, and `Settings/ConfigRules.cs` says what a valid file holds. A new version gets a recorded file in `tests/Thermi.Tests/Configs` and a migration.
- **The edits** the window makes are in `Editing/ItemEdits.cs`, `Editing/PowerEdits.cs`, `Editing/OverlayEdits.cs` and `Editing/StyleEdits.cs`: each gives valid settings, or the settings it was given and a `Refusal`, through the gate in `Editing/ConfigEdits.cs`.
- **Undo** is `Editing/UndoHistory.cs`: `Editing/ConfigDiff.cs` turns each edit into operations by item id (`Editing/UndoOperations.cs`) over the fields of `Editing/ConfigFields.cs`, and each is skipped when its target no longer holds what it wrote. `ConfigEdits.Replay` gives the same operations as a change of any settings, which is how the window writes an edit over what the file holds then. A new field of the settings goes into `Editing/ConfigFields.cs`, or undo cannot take it back; `UndoRulesTests` fails until it does.
- **Zero means "not read"** only for a CPU temperature, power or clock: `SensorMap.ZeroIsMissing` is the one rule, for metrics, sensor items and the catalog alike. `SensorMap.Named` gives every sensor the id an item names it by, a repeated library id included, and `SensorMap.Chosen` which sensor each metric reads, which the window names beside "Auto". `SensorNames` gives the name the window shows: the user's own from `sensorNames`, else a probable one for a Gigabyte board's ITE inputs, else the library's.
- **Network, disks and batteries**: a network's and a drive's speeds are `SensorKind.Throughput`, in bytes per second, and a battery's time remaining `SensorKind.Duration`; `SensorMap` follows the adapter that moved the most data, a physical one while any moves data (the snapshot lists the others), and the drive `Sensors/BusiestDrive.cs` picks once a minute from a minute's activity.
- **The window's sensors**: `Sensors/SensorCatalog.cs` lists every sensor by device with why it has no value, and finds them by words and synonyms, `Sensors/SensorHistory.cs` keeps ten minutes a second at a time in fixed rings, with the histogram and range the threshold track shows, `Look/MeterChart.cs` gives a meter's chart its scale, range, colors and push, and `Sensors/FanFinder.cs` decides when to load the CPU and which fan answered, with the fan rising most while it runs, its pick applied by `ItemEdits.UseCpuFan`. The load itself is the window's.
- **What the overlay draws** is decided here, so the window's stage and the overlay agree: `Look/OverlayLayout.cs` gives the items, each with its id so the stage knows which was clicked, and their pills from the layout and `own`, `Look/OverlayLook.cs` turns a style into sizes, weights, readable colors and where each pill goes, and `Placement/Snaps.cs` places the overlay from the monitors' plain geometry, each monitor named by `Placement/MonitorIds.cs` from the maker, model and serial of its EDID, or its connection, moved by `Placement/OledCare.cs` when OLED care is on, never writing the place it computes; a press moves it only past the drag distance, and `OverlayEdits.Release` writes only such a drag. The look also gives the font, the corners' radius, the outline and, under high contrast, Windows' colors, and `Look/CriticalGlow.cs` times the glow of an item going critical. The painter only draws. What a drag on the desktop does, the whole overlay moved or a pill dragged off with Ctrl, a drop on a snap mark (`Snaps.Near`) or into another overlay, and the corner's size, is `Placement/DesktopGestures.cs`.
- **Power items**: `Editing/PowerEdits.cs` combines sensors and sets the offset, the minimum, maximum or average, smoothing, decimals, graphs and alert-only, and `Look/ItemHistory.cs` keeps each item's last minute in fixed rings and its running minimum, maximum and average, kept by item, one for all the overlays and one in the window. `OverlayLayout.Build` takes it to show those values and the graphs.
- **Styles**: `Look/StyleFile.cs` reads and writes a `.thermi-style` file, the look alone, refusing a broken, newer or out-of-range one by the settings' own rules, and `Look/SavedStyles.cs` keeps the saved ones as files in a folder it is given, a file name made from any name. Renaming one renames it in the settings and in the undo steps.
- **Translations** are `Translations/Texts.resx`, English, built into Core itself, and `Texts.es.resx` beside it, built into a satellite assembly; MSBuild makes both with no package. `Translations/Texts.cs` gives them out: every text is a member of an enum marked `[Translated]` (`UiText`, in `Translations/UiText.cs`, `MetricLabel`, `MetricName`, `Refusal`, `NoValue` and more), keyed `Enum.Member`, so a new text is a new member and its line in every file, and `RepoRulesTests` checks both. `Texts.Pick` follows Windows' display language unless the settings choose one; numbers always follow the regional format the caller passes, whatever the language. `Texts.Pseudo` makes English 40 % longer, to test the window's room.
- **Temperatures** show in °C or °F (`ReadingFormatter`, `Temperatures`), and the file keeps °C.
- **When an overlay shows** is `Placement/OverlayVisibility.cs`: each overlay's own Hide, the "Hide overlays" flag that lives in memory, and each overlay's visibility, from what the overlay sees on its tick, and whether a full-screen app covers each overlay's monitor (`OverlayVisibility.FullScreen`, on the monitor `Snaps.ScreenOf` gives the overlay).
- **Hotkeys** are `Hotkeys.cs`: a combination as the file writes it, like `Ctrl+Alt+T`, read into what Windows registers or written from a key pressed with its modifiers (`Write`), and the "hotkey status" both processes agree on, the FNV-1a hash of the four combinations with the low bits of those that failed, so the window trusts only an answer about the hotkeys it shows.
- **The bridge** to the window's page is `Bridge/IWindowBridge.cs`, with the shapes it carries in `Bridge/BridgeTypes.cs` and `Bridge/BridgeViews.cs`, and the window's status, undo steps and About in `Bridge/WindowFacts.cs`; the build writes the page's TypeScript from them with `src/Thermi.Settings.Types`. `Bridge/BridgeCalls.cs` is its wire: a call by its camel-case name, its arguments and answer as JSON, choices as the file writes them. `Look/ChartHistory.cs` gives each chart its ten minutes, the overlay's moved on to now under the window's own.
- **The tray** is decided in `Tray/TrayScene.cs` and `Tray/TrayTexts.cs`: which icons show, their four-character texts, readable colors, tooltips cut to Windows' 127 characters, ids per install and menus, click-through only in thermi's own icon's; `Tray/TrayRefresh.cs` says which changed. `Tray/ThermiMark.cs` gives thermi's mark, its tooltip and each overlay's scene with its value tooltips, whose text is `Tray/ValueTooltips.cs`, `Tray/Menus.cs` both menus as data, and `Tray/Notices.cs` the Windows notices and "Notify me". Drawing them is the overlay's.

Start with `Sensors/SensorMap.cs` (which sensor feeds each metric) and `Look/OverlayLayout.cs` (what the overlay shows).
